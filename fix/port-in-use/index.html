Find what's using a port on Windows

“Port already in use” means another process is listening on it. Two commands find it, and a third frees it.

Find the processStop itWhen the owner looks strangeIs it reachable from your network?More fixes

Updated October 13, 2026

Find the process

In PowerShell, replace 3000 with your port:

Get-NetTCPConnection -LocalPort 3000 -State Listen |
  Select-Object LocalAddress, OwningProcess
Get-Process -Id <OwningProcess>

Or in Command Prompt, where the last column is the process ID:

netstat -ano | findstr :3000
tasklist /FI "PID eq <PID>"

Stop it

Stop-Process -Id <PID>

Or taskkill /PID <PID> /F in Command Prompt. Usually it's a dev server left running after its terminal closed, often node.exe or python.exe.

When the owner looks strange

  • wslrelay.exe or com.docker.backend.exe: the port belongs to something running inside WSL or a Docker container. Stop it there (docker ps, then docker stop).
  • System (PID 4): a Windows service such as IIS is using it.
  • Nothing is listening but you still get “access denied”: Hyper-V, WSL or Docker reserved a range of ports. List them with netsh interface ipv4 show excludedportrange protocol=tcp and pick a port outside those ranges.

Is it reachable from your network?

Check the LocalAddress. 127.0.0.1 or ::1 means only your PC can connect. 0.0.0.0 or :: means other devices on your network can reach it too, which you may not want on public Wi-Fi.
